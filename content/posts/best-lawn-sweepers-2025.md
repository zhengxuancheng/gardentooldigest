Raking is my least favorite yard chore, and I'm guessing it's yours too. It's the one job that eats an entire afternoon, hurts your back, and gets undone by a single gust of wind. A lawn sweeper does the same work in a fraction of the time. Hitch one behind a riding mower or ATV and you can clear clippings, leaves, and twigs across a full acre in about twenty minutes. For smaller yards, a push sweeper covers a modest lawn without bending over once.

Sweepers are simple machines: a set of spinning brushes flicks debris up into a hopper as you roll. There's no engine, no fuel, no belt to adjust on most models. The tradeoffs come down to width, hopper size, and how easy the thing is to dump. I tested six of the best, from a 26-inch push model to a 52-inch tow-behind, on a mix of oak leaves, grass clippings, and the occasional pine cone. Here's what I learned.

## What to Look For in a Lawn Sweeper

### Tow-Behind or Push

This is the first decision. Tow-behind sweepers hitch to a riding mower, lawn tractor, ATV, or even a UTV, and they're the right tool for anything bigger than a quarter acre. They cover ground fast, and the width (38 to 52 inches) matches your mower's deck. Push sweepers are exactly what they sound like: you walk behind them. They suit small lawns, and they double as a great way to pick up clippings without dragging out a big machine. If your yard is under a quarter acre and you don't own a tractor, start with a push model.

### Sweeping Width

Width is how much ground you cover per pass. A 38-inch sweeper matches a typical riding mower deck, so you can overlap your mowing path. A 42-inch sweeper is the sweet spot for most yards, and 50 to 52 inch models are for acreage where speed matters more than precision. The wider the sweeper, the harder it is to maneuver around trees and flower beds, so don't buy the widest one just because it exists.

### Hopper Capacity and Dumping

The hopper is the bag that catches everything, and capacity ranges from about 12 to 26 cubic feet. Bigger means fewer trips to the compost pile, but it also means more weight when full. Dumping is where sweepers differ: the best ones have a dump rope or a seat-mounted handle so you can empty the hopper without getting off the tractor. Flow-through hoppers use a mesh that lets air escape, which keeps leaves from clogging and lets you keep sweeping longer. If you're cleaning up a lot of leaves, a flow-through hopper is worth paying for.

### Brush Quality and Height Adjustment

The brushes do the actual work, so they matter more than the frame. Look for polypropylene brushes that stay stiff in the heat and don't shed bristles. Height adjustment lets you match the brush to your surface: lower for tight Bermuda grass, higher for thick fescue or gravel. The easiest systems adjust with a single lever from the seat. Fixed-height sweepers are cheaper but fussier to get right.

### Build Quality and Storage

Sweepers take a beating, bouncing over uneven ground with a full hopper. Steel frames with welded joints hold up; the cheap ones flex and rattle apart within a season or two. Pneumatic tires with sealed bearings roll smoothly and last. Storage matters too: the best models fold up or tip up vertically to lean against a garage wall, which keeps a 6-foot machine from becoming a permanent lawn ornament.

## FAQ

### Tow-behind or push lawn sweeper: which do I need?

If you own a riding mower or ATV and your lawn is bigger than a quarter acre, get a tow-behind. It does the job in a fraction of the time. If your yard is small or you just want to pick up clippings without machinery, a push sweeper is simpler, cheaper, and stores flat. Most people with a tractor should skip the push model entirely.

### Can a lawn sweeper pick up wet leaves?

It can, but it won't love it. Wet leaves clump and clog standard hoppers fast, which is why flow-through mesh hoppers exist. The Agri-Fab 44-inch with the flow-through hopper handles damp leaves far better than any standard bag. If you're sweeping after rain, wait for the leaves to dry a little if you can, and empty the hopper more often.

### Will a lawn sweeper work with an ATV or zero-turn mower?

Yes. Tow-behind sweepers hitch to any standard trailer hitch, and both ATVs and zero-turns have them. For ATVs, check the recommended tow speed, usually 5 to 10 mph. Zero-turns work fine, but keep in mind the sweeper follows your turning path, so tight maneuvers take a wider swing than the mower alone.

### How do I maintain a lawn sweeper?

Keep the brushes free of wrapped string and twine, check the tires for pressure, and grease the wheel bearings once a season. After each use, empty the hopper so debris doesn't rot inside. If the brushes wear down over the years, most brands sell replacements that bolt on in minutes. That's the whole maintenance list, which is the appeal of a sweeper over a powered machine.

### Do lawn sweepers work on grass clippings?

Very well, and that's one of their best uses. Sweeping clippings after mowing keeps the lawn looking groomed and prevents clumps of cut grass from smothering the turf. It also collects them before they get tracked into the house. A sweeper used after every mow is the easiest way to keep a yard looking manicured without bagging on the mower.
